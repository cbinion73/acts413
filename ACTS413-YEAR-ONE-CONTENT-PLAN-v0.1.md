# Acts 4:13 Year One Content and Release Plan v0.1

**Planning period:** August 2026–July 2027  
**Operating model:** Foyer → Living Rooms → Kitchen  
**Strategic companion:** *Acts 4:13 Marketing Plan v0.2*  
**Status:** Working plan for founder review; product dates are readiness-gated

## 1. Year One Objective

Year One should prove that Acts 4:13 can become a trusted, repeatable formation rhythm—not merely launch a website, sell a book, or accumulate an audience.

The year succeeds when people:

1. Recognize that Acts 4:13 understands something they are carrying.
2. Enter through Ordinary, Courageous, or Transformed.
3. Complete a small practice rather than merely consume content.
4. Return for a coherent weekly rhythm.
5. Voluntarily become known through an account, reply, prayer, group, or story.
6. Take a courageous step, love someone well, repair something, or serve.
7. Choose a deeper book, tool, group, or experience because it naturally continues their journey.

The editorial promise is simple:

> Every week, Acts 4:13 will help an ordinary person spend time with Jesus and carry one faithful practice into real life.

## 2. Planning Assumptions

This calendar uses the following assumptions until the founder replaces them with confirmed facts:

- Year One begins August 1, 2026.
- Acts413.com and email are the primary relationship channels.
- One strong weekly content package is sustainable; the same source material is adapted across channels.
- Dust is the first flagship Kitchen offering and the first guided formation journey.
- Dust's release date is not yet treated as fixed. October 2026 is a target window only if the release gate is passed.
- Chronicle receives a research, waitlist, beta, and launch runway. June 2027 is an early-access readiness window; July 2027 is a target public-release window only if product, privacy, support, and onboarding gates are passed.
- The second Acts 4:13 book is developed and audience-tested during Year One; publication is not promised before manuscript readiness is known.
- Prayer moderation, story consent, Scripture permissions, and safety escalation are part of content operations—not post-launch administration.
- Acts 4:13 begins with a standard production level and may contract to the minimum viable level if quality or care begins to suffer.

## 4. Content Architecture

### The five jobs of content

Every published item must perform at least one job:

1. **Recognition:** “Someone understands where I am.”
2. **Encounter:** “I have made room to notice Jesus.”
3. **Practice:** “I know what to do next.”
4. **Relationship:** “I can return, respond, pray, or walk with someone.”
5. **Continuation:** “There is a deeper path when I am ready.”

Content that performs none of these jobs should not be published merely to maintain frequency.

### Core content types

| Type | Primary job | Typical length | Best home | Required integrity check |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Story | Recognition | 3–7 minutes | Foyer + Living Room | Real, permissioned, not exaggerated |
| Testimony | Recognition + proof | 3–8 minutes | Foyer + Living Room | Informed consent; Jesus and fruit remain central |
| Scripture moment | Encounter | 1–5 minutes | Foyer + all rooms | Translation, attribution, and quotation rights |
| Reflection | Encounter | 3–7 minutes | Living Room + email | Distinguish Scripture, interpretation, and application |
| Prayer | Encounter + relationship | 1–4 minutes | Prayer Room + email | Sensitive language and pastoral boundaries |
| Practice | Practice | 5 minutes–7 days | Living Room | Simple, safe, and possible in ordinary life |
| Guided study | Practice | 15–45 minutes | Living Room / group | Clear context, questions, and facilitator notes |
| Downloadable guide | Practice + return | 1–12 pages | Living Room | Concrete use; accessible formatting; version owner |
| Book excerpt | Recognition + continuation | 1–5 minutes | Foyer / Kitchen bridge | Rights and approved excerpt boundaries |
| Quote | Recognition | Under 60 seconds | Foyer / social | Exact source; no misattribution |
| Conversation prompt | Relationship | 5–20 minutes | Group / community | Hospitable and non-coercive |
| Audio meditation | Encounter | 3–12 minutes | Chapel / email | Calm production; transcript available |
| Video story or teaching | Recognition + practice | 3–12 minutes | YouTube / site | Complete value without requiring a click |
| Live conversation | Relationship | 30–60 minutes | Site / video platform | Host, moderation, replay, and next step |
| Service invitation | Courage + service | Variable | Courageous / community | Specific, ethical, locally appropriate |
| Product education | Continuation | 2–8 minutes | Kitchen + email | Explain value without spiritual pressure |
| Community report | Trust | 3–8 minutes | Foyer / email | Transparent outcomes without exploiting stories |

### Content record requirements

Each item in the content system should carry:

- Content ID and working title
- Content type and format
- Primary Living Room
- Secondary room, if any
- Formation movement: presence, worth, way, together, courage, service, recognition
- Felt need or question
- Intended audience and journey stage
- One-sentence promise
- One primary next step
- Estimated time required
- Access level: public, account, group, or paid
- Source, author, Scripture translation, and rights status
- Story/testimony consent status
- Pastoral, safety, or moderation review status
- Owner, reviewer, production status, and version
- Publish date, feature date, and retire/review date
- Email, social, audio, video, and printable derivatives
- Related free resource and optional Kitchen continuation
- Success signal and actual result

## 5. Sustainable Publishing Cadence

### Standard weekly package

Create one source package each week and adapt it deliberately:

- **Foyer:** one featured moment
- **Email:** one formation letter with one practice
- **Living Room:** one permanent or time-bound resource placement
- **Social:** three useful adaptations—recognition, story/teaching, and practice/prayer
- **Community:** one question, prayer, or response invitation
- **Kitchen:** at most one contextual continuation when genuinely relevant

### Monthly package

- One substantial free giveaway
- One real participant or historical story
- One audio meditation or long-form video
- One group conversation guide
- One prayer emphasis
- One service or courageous-action invitation
- One restrained Kitchen invitation
- One editorial and metrics review

### Quarterly package

- One multi-day formation journey
- One live founder/community conversation
- One group or cohort opportunity
- One service activation
- One participant listening round
- One transparent “what we are learning” update

### Annual standard production count

| Asset | Year One target |
|---|---:|
| Weekly source packages | 48 |
| Formation emails | 48–52 |
| Foyer feature changes | 48 |
| Social adaptations | 144 |
| Monthly giveaways | 12 |
| Long-form videos or audio pieces | 12 |
| Permissioned stories/testimonies | 12 |
| Group conversation guides | 12 |
| Guided multi-day journeys | 4 |
| Live conversations | 4 |
| Service activations | 4 |
| Major Kitchen releases | 1–2, readiness-gated |

Four weeks remain intentionally lighter for rest, review, emergencies, and seasonal transitions. The system should never require thin content simply to reach a number.

### Capacity levels

**Minimum viable:** one weekly source package, one email, two social adaptations, one monthly giveaway, one quarterly journey.  
**Standard:** the Year One cadence above.  
**Expanded:** add a second weekly video/audio piece, more live interaction, and paid promotion only after standard cadence is stable for eight weeks.

## 9. Chronicle Release Plan

### Role

Chronicle is the durable private tool for keeping Scripture, prayer, notes, and the story of a life with God connected over years. It should be introduced only after Acts 4:13 has demonstrated trustworthy care in the lighter web-based companion.

### Narrative progression

1. **Problem awareness:** meaningful prayers, notes, and moments become scattered or forgotten.
2. **Practice awareness:** remembering and noticing are part of formation.
3. **Product principle:** a private, durable place can help carry that story without replacing relationship or church.
4. **Trust proof:** privacy, ownership, export, consent, and boundaries are clear.
5. **Invitation:** move into Chronicle only by explicit choice.

### Year One runway

| Period | Chronicle work | Public content |
|---|---|---|
| Jan–Feb | Discovery interviews and workflow mapping | Remembering, noticing, prayer history, and scattered notes |
| Mar | Closed alpha; define support and privacy expectations | Why private reflection differs from a public community feed |
| Apr | Refine onboarding and core use cases | Thirty Days of Noticing Jesus |
| May | Bounded beta and feedback | Product principles, founder use, and honest beta learning |
| Jun | Readiness review and bounded early access if gates pass | Demonstrations, use-case stories, pricing, migration consent |
| Jul | Public-release window or continued beta | Year-in-review story map and durable continuation |

### Chronicle launch assets

- Product promise and non-promise
- Founder use story
- Three use-case demonstrations: prayer, Scripture/sermon notes, and life-story connections
- Privacy and data ownership page in plain language
- Export, deletion, and cancellation explanation
- Web Companion versus Chronicle comparison
- Explicit transfer-consent flow
- Guided first-week onboarding
- Support and troubleshooting materials
- Beta feedback summary and known limitations
- Pricing and refund terms without hidden recurrence

### Chronicle release gate

- Core workflows are reliable with real beta users
- Privacy, security, ownership, retention, deletion, and export behavior are documented and tested
- Nothing moves from Acts 4:13 into Chronicle without explicit user action
- Onboarding produces a successful first use without founder assistance
- Pricing, billing, cancellation, support, and refunds are ready
- Product analytics do not expose prayer content or spiritual vulnerability
- Accessibility and mobile experience meet the launch standard
- At least ten beta users choose to continue using it after the novelty period

If any gate fails, the calendar continues with formation content and beta learning. Marketing never creates a deadline the product cannot honor.

### Series territories

Future books should emerge from a real dimension of Acts 4:13:

- Presence
- Ordinary worth
- Formation
- Together
- Courage
- Recognition
- Astonishment and impact

### Year One book-development rhythm

| Quarter | Work | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Capture recurring questions and phrases from the founding audience | Search, reply, room-choice, interview, and prayer-theme patterns without exposing private content |
| Q2 | Select two candidate territories and publish original reflections/practices | Sustained engagement and qualitative resonance, not one viral post |
| Q3 | Test two short series and conduct reader conversations | Participants practice, return, and ask for deeper treatment |
| Q4 | Choose one book direction; draft concept, promise, reader, chapter arc, and sample | Clear differentiation from Dust and a credible manuscript path |

### Book concept gate

A future book proceeds when:

- It answers a recurring human question the community actually expresses.
- It explores a distinct Acts 4:13 territory rather than repackaging Dust.
- It includes truth, practice, conversation, and courageous love/service.
- Chris has a genuine contribution, not merely a market opportunity.
- The concept can support individual and group formation.
- The book's role in the larger journey is clear before title or cover development begins.

No Year One marketing calendar should promise a second publication date before manuscript and publishing readiness are known.

## 11. Three-Year Portfolio Horizon

This horizon coordinates future work without turning possibilities into promises.

### Year One — prove the formation system

- Establish the Foyer, Living Rooms, prayer/account relationship, and editorial rhythm.
- Launch and support Dust as the foundational formation doorway.
- Release Chronicle only after trust and product gates pass.
- Pilot groups, service activations, and facilitator support.
- Select and validate the territory for Book 2.

### Year Two — deepen and multiply

- Publish Book 2 only if manuscript and audience evidence are ready.
- Build a full individual and group journey around Book 2.
- Mature Chronicle through real retention, support, export, and privacy learning.
- Establish recurring facilitator cohorts and church/group packs.
- Create a dependable annual formation calendar that does not depend on constant founder output.
- Test one annual in-person or hybrid gathering if the community asks for it and operational care is available.

### Year Three — expand carefully

- Consider Book 3 from the next validated Acts 4:13 territory.
- License or distribute group resources more broadly to churches and partner organizations.
- Develop trained companion/facilitator pathways with explicit authority and care boundaries.
- Expand Chronicle integrations or platforms only when the core private experience is durable.
- Consider additional retreats, cohorts, or service partnerships based on measured formation and operational capacity.

### Portfolio sequencing rule

No new book, app feature, course, cohort, or event enters active launch production unless:

1. Its role in the formation journey is distinct.
2. An owner and support model exist.
3. The current release is stable enough not to be abandoned.
4. Free formation remains meaningful.
5. The release calendar has capacity without sacrificing editorial quality, prayer care, or customer support.

**Email:** the primary weekly relationship and guided-journey channel.  
**Account:** private continuity—saved resources, prayers, notes, and preferences.

### Discovery channels

Use current channel assumptions provisionally:

- **YouTube:** broad adult reach, searchable teaching, stories, and audio/video meditations.
- **Facebook:** broad adult and group reach, especially if the founding audience is middle-aged or older.
- **Instagram:** visual excerpts, practices, and short video if the audience skews younger or existing reach justifies it.
- **Podcast feeds:** only after the monthly audio/video rhythm proves sustainable; do not create a separate content burden prematurely.

Pew Research Center's 2025 U.S. data shows YouTube and Facebook remain the only major platforms used by a majority in every adult age group, while Instagram is especially strong among adults under 50. This supports starting narrow with YouTube, Facebook, and owned email rather than attempting every platform at once.

Repurposing changes format, opening, and length; it should not produce identical copy pasted everywhere.

## 13. Production Workflow

### Six-week monthly production cycle

| Timing | Work |
|---|---|
| Six weeks before | Confirm monthly human question, formation movement, giveaway, story need, and Kitchen connection |
| Five weeks before | Research Scripture/context; request stories; confirm rights and guests |
| Four weeks before | Draft source packages, giveaway, group guide, and journey assets |
| Three weeks before | Theological/editorial review; pastoral and safety review where needed |
| Two weeks before | Design, audio/video production, accessibility, links, forms, and metadata |
| One week before | User test, proof, schedule, consent check, analytics check, and fallback plan |
| Publication week | Publish, respond, moderate, support, and record live issues |
| Following week | Review behavior and qualitative responses; fix and document learning |

## 14. Editorial, Rights, and Care Governance

### Story and testimony

- Never invent, composite, or materially embellish a testimony.
- Capture what may be used, where, for how long, whether a name/image may appear, and how consent may be withdrawn.
- Avoid framing suffering as proof that a product works.
- Reconfirm consent before reusing a story in a paid campaign.

### Scripture and quotations

- Choose approved Bible translations and maintain their attribution and quotation limits.
- Record translation and rights status on every Scripture item.
- Verify every quote against a reliable original source.
- Use book excerpts only with explicit rights or within a documented, rights-aware boundary.

### Prayer and sensitive information

- Prayer submissions default to private handling unless the person chooses otherwise.
- Public prayers are moderated before publication and identifying details are removed as needed.
- “I prayed for this” is the primary public response; no public advice, counseling, or debate.
- Prayer-team follow-up, marketing consent, public sharing, and account creation remain separate.
- Urgent-risk language points people toward immediate local or professional support.

### AI use

- AI may assist with outlines, repurposing, metadata, accessibility, and production operations.
- AI does not fabricate personal stories, spiritual experiences, quotations, pastoral authority, or claims about what God told someone.
- Human review is required for Scripture interpretation, prayer, testimony, crisis language, and public theological claims.
