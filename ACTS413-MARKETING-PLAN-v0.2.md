# Acts 4:13 Marketing Plan v0.2

**Working strategy for founder review**  
**Prepared:** July 28, 2026  
**Strategic sources:** *Acts 4:13 Website Plan* and *Acts 4:13 Foundational Brand Document v0.1*

## 1. The Marketing Decision

Acts 4:13 should not enter the world as another Christian content platform, online store, or founder-led media brand. It should enter as a clear, generous invitation:

> Spend time with Jesus. Practice His way. Take the next courageous step.

The first marketing job is not to explain everything Acts 4:13 may eventually become. It is to help one ordinary person experience one honest moment with Jesus, take one meaningful step, and want to return.

The first public campaign should therefore be built around a **Dust-based formation journey**, with Acts413.com serving as its home. Dust supplies the doorway; Acts 4:13 supplies the enduring journey and community.

### Founder-validated marketing principles

The earlier website-design conversation established several decisions that now govern this plan:

- **Recognition before explanation:** the first emotional response should be “This understands something I am carrying,” not “I understand this organization.”
- **Felt need before framework language:** visitors choose “I need to remember who I am,” “I need courage for my next step,” or “I want to be changed from the inside out” before being introduced to Ordinary, Courageous, and Transformed.
- **Movement before choice:** a story, excerpt, Scripture, testimony, or prayer should move the visitor before the site asks them to select a room.
- **Generosity before information:** reading remains open; registration appears when it creates a genuine benefit such as delivering, saving, or continuing something meaningful.
- **Relationship before segmentation:** first name, email, and chosen room begin a mutual relationship. Deeper questions are asked progressively and voluntarily.
- **Care before conversion:** prayer is never a lead-generation mechanism. Prayer follow-up, encouragement email, public sharing, and account creation are distinct choices.
- **One coherent home:** one Acts 4:13 account can eventually carry downloads, saved resources, prayers, notes, practices, and purchases without forcing login at the front door.
- **Curated life, not content volume:** the site feels alive because each week’s material is intentionally connected to what people may be carrying—not because a feed is constantly filled.

## 2. What We Are Marketing

We are marketing neither access to Chris nor a stream of inspirational material. We are offering a trustworthy path from spiritual hunger to practiced faithfulness:

1. **Be seen:** “I do not have to be impressive, credentialed, or certain to begin.”
2. **Encounter Jesus:** receive a story, Scripture, reflection, or prayer that creates attention.
3. **Practice His way:** take a small, concrete action in ordinary life.
4. **Return:** continue through a coherent rhythm rather than an endless feed.
5. **Walk with others:** become known, encouraged, and strengthened.
6. **Act courageously:** take the next faithful step while fear is still present.
7. **Love and serve:** let formation become recognizable fruit.

This is the marketing funnel, but it should never feel like a funnel to the person experiencing it. It should feel like hospitality.

## 3. Primary Audience

### First audience: the spiritually hungry but stalled Christian

This person believes in Jesus but feels hurried, distant, disappointed, numb, or trapped in religious performance. They do not need more content volume. They need permission to slow down, an honest place to begin, and a practice small enough to live.

**What they feel:** “I know about Jesus, but I do not feel close to Him.”  
**What they need:** a gentle first encounter and a repeatable rhythm.  
**Best doorway:** Dust, a short practice, or the Transformed Living Room.  
**Desired first action:** begin a seven-day journey or receive a weekly formation email.

### Second audience: the overlooked or underqualified ordinary person

This person wonders whether their life can matter because of failure, lack of credentials, age, uncertainty, or comparison.

**What they feel:** “People like me are not the people God uses.”  
**What they need:** dignity before achievement and stories of recognizable faithfulness.  
**Best doorway:** the Ordinary Living Room and testimony-led content.  
**Desired first action:** choose an identity-focused practice or download a free guide.

### Third audience: the person facing a real next step

This person is dealing with a hard conversation, calling, loss, leadership decision, act of service, or necessary change.

**What they feel:** “I know something needs to happen, but I am afraid.”  
**What they need:** courage with humility, prayer, and companionship.  
**Best doorway:** the Courageous Living Room.  
**Desired first action:** name the next faithful step and invite support.

### Multiplier audience: trusted group and church leaders

Pastors, small-group leaders, ministry leaders, and mature companions can introduce the journey to others. They should be served with a clear group pathway and facilitator materials, while Acts 4:13 remains explicitly a companion to the local church rather than a replacement for it.

## 4. Positioning and Message Hierarchy

### Positioning

**A Jesus-centered formation community for ordinary people ready to live courageously.**

### Message hierarchy

1. **Human truth:** You do not have to be impressive, polished, or certain to begin.
2. **Invitation:** Spend time with Jesus.
3. **Practice:** Learn and practice His way in ordinary life.
4. **Fruit:** Take the next courageous step in love and service.
5. **Proof:** Over time, a life with Jesus leaves recognizable evidence.

### Lead messages to test

**Homepage / broad invitation**  
They recognized that they had been with Jesus. Maybe that is where your story begins, too.

**Narrative bridge**  
They had been with Jesus. And then they kept walking.

**Dust journey**  
You can know a great deal about Jesus and still feel far from Him. Dust is an invitation to come close enough to be changed.

**Ordinary**  
You are more than what you have done, what has happened to you, or what others have called you.

**Courageous**  
Courage is not having no fear. It is choosing faithfulness while fear is still present.

**Transformed**  
Transformation is not becoming someone else. It is becoming more fully alive in Christ.

These are campaign and pathway messages, not permanent taglines. The foundational document correctly leaves the permanent tagline open until the homepage and visual identity are tested.

### The 15-second message sequence

The homepage should earn attention in four quick movements:

1. **Emotion:** a bright, human image makes the visitor want to remain.
2. **Meaning:** one short Acts 4:13-centered thought establishes the premise.
3. **Recognition:** a story or line reflects something real in the visitor’s life.
4. **Action:** one felt-need choice opens the appropriate Living Room.

The hero should not contain a manifesto, email form, product pitch, or institutional welcome. The framework labels can be taught after the visitor has recognized their need.

## 5. The Offer and Relationship Ladder

### The Foyer — public generosity

- A changing story, Scripture, reflection, testimony, or prayer before the room choice
- A short “try this now” practice
- Three felt-need invitations into Ordinary, Courageous, and Transformed
- A gentle prayer invitation
- A clear reason to return

**Primary conversion:** choose a Living Room, begin the Dust journey, or subscribe to one useful rhythm.

### The Living Rooms — free formation

- A curated Start Here sequence for each room
- Three to five strong launch pieces per room
- One downloadable practice per room
- Prayer, Bible study, reflection, and saved resources
- A lightweight personal companion for logged-in participants

**Primary conversion:** complete a first practice, save something meaningful, return, and voluntarily become known.

### The Kitchen — compelling paid depth

- Dust and future books
- Small-group studies and facilitator resources
- Chronicle and other durable personal tools
- Cohorts, workshops, retreats, and community experiences
- Contributor and service pathways

**Primary conversion:** choose a longer journey that offers genuine added value.

### Governing Kitchen rule

The earlier conversation explicitly defined the Living Rooms as generous free help and the Kitchen as compelling things for sale. The foundational brand document also requires that belonging never become a paid spiritual hierarchy. Together they produce this rule:

> Belonging, prayer, basic formation, and the path to service remain available without purchase. Payment applies to substantial products, durable tools, facilitation, production, or guided experiences—not spiritual status or access to Jesus.

Community pages may live in the Kitchen when they belong to a paid cohort, course, or facilitated experience. The broader Acts 4:13 relationship and community invitation remain free.

## 6. Launch Campaign: “Been With Jesus”

“Been With Jesus” is a proposed launch campaign name, not the brand tagline. It directly expresses the founding verse while leaving room for Dust to serve as the first guided experience.

### Campaign promise

Seven days to slow down, meet Jesus in Scripture and ordinary life, and notice the next faithful step He may be forming in you.

### Free entry experience

**The Seven-Day Been With Jesus Journey**

Each day contains:

1. A brief scene or excerpt from Dust
2. A short Scripture passage
3. One honest question: “What are you noticing?”
4. One practice that can be completed that day
5. One optional prayer
6. One invitation to reflect, reply, or carry the practice forward

Day seven ends with three possible next steps:

- Continue in Ordinary, Courageous, or Transformed
- Read or purchase Dust
- Join or bring the journey to a small group

### Why this should lead the launch

- It lets people experience the brand before being asked to understand it.
- It turns the core conviction into a practiced claim rather than an advertising slogan.
- It creates a natural return rhythm and a respectful email relationship.
- It can work for individuals, groups, churches, and book readers.
- It creates evidence about which Living Room and needs resonate most strongly.

### Visual and emotional territory

The Dust cover resolved an earlier visual false start. The launch should feel like the best part of the morning:

- Bright white, warm sand, sunlight, fabric, dust, and human movement
- Spacious, alive, and forward-moving rather than dark, heavy, or ominous
- Elegant editorial typography with restrained earth-and-courage accents
- Honest people already walking, working, gathering, praying, or serving
- Dust presented as the first deeper continuation, not as the reason the whole site exists

Avoid dark cinematic water as the dominant world, generic church imagery, visual spectacle, oversized abstract declarations, and large blocks of intimidating language.

## 7. Channel Strategy

### 1. Acts413.com — the canonical home

Every campaign should resolve to one owned home, not scatter the journey across social platforms. The homepage introduces the story; dedicated pathway pages help visitors act.

### 2. Email — the primary relationship channel

Email should feel like a companion arriving with one useful thing, not a newsletter announcing everything the organization has done.

Recommended rhythm:

- One weekly formation letter
- Short journey emails during a defined series
- Occasional product or experience invitations connected to the formation theme already underway
- Explicit, separate consent for prayer follow-up and marketing

The first useful profile should remain small: first name, email, and the Living Room most relevant today. Later questions—what someone is carrying, what encouragement would help, or whether prayer follow-up is desired—should emerge only when context makes them helpful.

### 3. Founder voice — human trust, carefully bounded

Chris should be visible as founder, author, and fellow participant, but not positioned as the destination. Use short personal video, written reflections, and honest stories that continually redirect attention toward Jesus and the participant’s own practice.

### 4. Participant stories — proof through fruit

Stories should emphasize courage, repair, generosity, faithfulness, love, and service. They require meaningful consent and should never exaggerate a sacred story into sales proof.

### 5. Church and group partnerships — trusted multiplication

Offer a simple host kit for pastors, small-group leaders, friends, and workplace or neighborhood companions:

- Seven-day journey overview
- Discussion prompts
- Facilitator notes and boundaries
- Printable invitation
- Group registration page
- Clear “companion to the church” explanation

### 6. Social and video — discovery, not the home

Use brief, native pieces that carry one complete idea: a scene, question, practice, prayer, or story. Every piece should be useful even if the viewer never clicks. Calls to action should invite the next step without manufactured urgency.

Initial platforms should be selected after reviewing Chris’s current audience, reach, and production capacity. The strategy does not require being everywhere.

## 8. Editorial System

### Weekly formation rhythm

Choose one human burden or seasonally relevant question each week. Build one coherent set around it:

- **Foyer:** one featured story, excerpt, Scripture, or prayer
- **Email:** one formation letter and practice
- **Social:** three adaptations—a question, a brief teaching or story, and a practice
- **Community:** one reflection prompt or prayer invitation
- **Pathway:** one clear invitation into the relevant Living Room

Every item should also carry operational metadata: content type, primary room, theme, time required, season, journey stage, visibility, publish/retire dates, and any related free or paid continuation. One strong piece can then serve the Foyer, remain in its Living Room, support an email, and connect naturally to a deeper offering.

### Monthly rhythm

- One substantial free Living Room resource
- One participant or community story
- One group or service invitation
- One Kitchen invitation that genuinely extends the month’s formation theme

### Content test

Before publishing, ask:

1. Does this help someone attend to Jesus or practice His way?
2. Does it respect worth before performance?
3. Is there a concrete but non-coercive next step?
4. Does it move beyond consumption toward courage, love, community, or service?
5. Is Jesus the center, or has the platform, founder, or product quietly become the hero?

## 9. The First 90 Days

### Days 1–15: establish the launch foundation

- Founder-review and approve the mission, audience priority, and message hierarchy
- Complete preliminary name and trademark-confusion review before major public investment
- Choose the launch campaign name after testing “Been With Jesus” against two alternatives
- Define the seven-day Dust journey and draft all seven days
- Create the homepage, campaign landing page, and thank-you/next-step page
- Build the welcome and journey email sequence
- Prepare a simple group host kit
- Recruit a founding test circle of 15–30 people

### Days 16–30: private pilot

- Run the seven-day journey with the founding test circle
- Conduct five to eight short participant conversations
- Observe where people stop, respond, return, pray, or ask for help
- Revise language and practices based on lived response
- Collect only stories for which informed permission has been given
- Confirm moderation and pastoral/safety escalation processes before opening prayer features

### Days 31–60: public founding launch

- Publish the Foyer and seven-day journey
- Release a founder story explaining why Acts 4:13 exists
- Invite the current trusted audience personally before using paid reach
- Equip the first five to ten group hosts
- Publish one coherent weekly formation theme
- Offer Dust as the natural continuation, with no false urgency

### Days 61–90: deepen what is working

- Identify which Living Room attracts the strongest qualified response
- Build its Start Here sequence first while preserving access to all three rooms
- Publish two participant stories centered on recognizable fruit
- Run the first guided group or cohort
- Test one modest Kitchen offer connected to the active formation theme
- Decide whether any paid audience acquisition is justified by retention and practice data

## 10. Measurement

Traffic and list size matter only as supporting indicators. The main measurement question is: **Are people moving from encounter to practice, return, relationship, courage, and service?**

### Core measures

- **Qualified arrival:** visitors reaching the Foyer or campaign from an intended source
- **Pathway choice:** percentage choosing Ordinary, Courageous, or Transformed
- **Fifteen-second comprehension:** whether a first-time visitor can identify what the site offers and their next step without reading an explanation
- **Journey start:** percentage beginning the seven-day experience
- **Practice completion:** self-reported or behaviorally confirmed completion where appropriate
- **Return:** seven-, fourteen-, and thirty-day return rate
- **Relationship permission:** voluntary email consent, room choice, saved resource, or prayer follow-up consent
- **Journey completion:** percentage reaching day seven
- **Next step:** continuation into a Living Room, group, Dust, or another practice
- **Formation evidence:** permissioned reports of courage, repair, generosity, love, faithfulness, or service
- **Trust health:** unsubscribes, complaints, prayer moderation incidents, consent problems, and cancellation friction
- **Sustainable revenue:** purchases or registrations that follow genuine engagement, tracked without treating spiritual activity as a sales score

### Initial 90-day learning targets

Numeric targets should be set after the pilot establishes a baseline. During the first 90 days, prioritize evidence that:

- People can explain what Acts 4:13 is in their own words.
- The seven-day journey is completed and shared without pressure.
- At least one Living Room need is clearly validated.
- Participants report specific practices or courageous next steps.
- Group hosts can lead the experience without dependence on Chris.
- A paid continuation can be offered without weakening trust.

## 11. Guardrails

- Never hide Jesus to increase reach.
- Never use Jesus, prayer, fear, shame, or promised transformation to force a sale.
- Never place basic belonging or spiritual status behind a paywall.
- Never add prayer requests to marketing without explicit consent.
- Never present private behavior, prayer content, or spiritual vulnerability as targeting data.
- Never make Chris, Dust, Chronicle, or the platform the hero of the participant’s story.
- Never use false scarcity, countdown pressure, or exaggerated testimony.
- Keep the public path understandable to curious people without disguising the destination.
- Encourage appropriate church, pastoral, professional, or emergency support when the platform is not the right source of care.

## 12. Decisions Needed to Move to v0.2

1. **Launch center:** Is Dust ready to anchor the first public journey, and what is its expected publication window?
2. **Existing reach:** What audiences, email lists, church relationships, podcast/video channels, and social accounts already exist?
3. **Founder role:** How visible does Chris want to be in writing, video, live teaching, and community interaction?
4. **Launch capacity:** What budget and weekly production capacity are realistic for the first 90 days?
5. **Primary outcome:** Is the first launch optimizing for founding participants, Dust readership, email relationships, group adoption, or early sustainable revenue?
6. **Public timing:** Will the name-confusion review be completed before a broad launch?
